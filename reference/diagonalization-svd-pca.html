Diagonalization, SVD and PCA

Find the useful axes, then decide how many coordinates to keep.

Diagonalization uses one basis

A = PDP⁻¹. Put independent eigenvectors in the columns of P and their eigenvalues in the matching diagonal slots of D. Read from the right. P⁻¹ finds the input's coordinates in the eigenvector basis, D scales them and P rebuilds the output. An n × n matrix needs n independent eigenvectors.

For a real symmetric matrix, choose an orthonormal eigenvector matrix Q. Then Q⁻¹ = Qᵀ. Each row of Qᵀ gives one coordinate by taking a dot product. Lesson 1.32

SVD uses input and output axes

A = UΣVᵀ. The columns of V are the input axes, and the columns of U are the output axes. For A of shape m × n, the full factors U, Σ and Vᵀ have shapes m × m, m × n and n × n.

  1. Find orthonormal eigenvectors vᵢ of AᵀA. Order them by eigenvalue λᵢ from largest to smallest.
  2. Put σᵢ = √λᵢ in the min(m, n) diagonal slots of Σ.
  3. For σᵢ > 0, compute uᵢ = Avᵢ/σᵢ. For zero values, complete the axes with perpendicular unit vectors.

Rank-k approximation. Add the first k pieces σᵢuᵢvᵢᵀ. The sum of the squares of the dropped singular values gives the squared Frobenius error, which is the sum of squares of all entries of the residual matrix. Lesson 1.35

PCA finds where centered data varies most

  1. With rows as observations and columns as features, subtract each feature mean from its column. Call the centered data Xc.
  2. For n rows, use the sample covariance matrix C = XcᵀXc/(n − 1). Its diagonal shows feature variances and its off-diagonal shows paired covariances.
  3. Find the unit eigenvectors of C in descending eigenvalue order. For each row, dot its centered values with each retained direction to get its scores.
  4. For positive total variance, divide a direction's eigenvalue by the sum across all directions to get its explained variance ratio. To reconstruct a row, add the scored directions and then the original mean.

Using SVD. The right singular vectors of Xc are the PCA axes. The variance along axis i is σᵢ²/(n − 1). Lesson 1.37
