Conditional probability

Read P(A | B) as “the probability of A given B.” Start with the group named after the bar.

In a table of counts, find the cell where A and B overlap. Divide that count by the total for B.

P(A | B) = count of A and B ÷ count of B

If you have probabilities instead of counts, divide P(A and B) by P(B). The calculation requires a positive denominator.

Example. Of 100 learners, 40 studied online and 24 of those completed the course. The completion rate among online learners is 24/40 = 60%. The share of all learners who studied online and completed is 24/100 = 24%.

Switching the condition from B to A keeps the overlap and changes which group supplies the denominator.
