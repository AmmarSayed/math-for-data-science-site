Slope

Slope tells you how much y changes per unit increase in x along a line.

Slope = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)

Subtract the same starting point from the same ending point in both coordinates. The units are units of y per unit of x.

Moving right, a rising line has positive slope, a falling line has negative slope, and a horizontal line has zero slope. A vertical line has undefined slope because the change in x is zero.

Example. From (2, 10) to (6, 8), the change is −2 vertically and +4 horizontally. The slope is −2/4 = −1/2.

In the form y = mx + b, m is the slope and b is the output at x = 0.
