Matrix times vector: one dot product per row

You already know the dot product and the size rule for matrices. Put them together and you can compute a whole column of results at once. A regression model uses this step to make its predictions.

Revenue for every customer at once

Each row of X records how many units one customer bought of products A and B. The vector w holds the prices: 3 for A and 2 for B.

To find what customer 2 paid, take the dot product of row 2 with the prices:

(4, 3) · (3, 2) = 4·3 + 3·2 = 18

Do the same for every row and you get Xw = (8, 18, 13), one total per customer. That's the whole operation: entry i of Xw is row i of X dotted with w.

The size rule from your diagnostic explains the shape. A (3×2) matrix times a (2×1) vector gives (3×1). The inner 2s must match, because each row needs exactly one weight per column. The outer 3 tells you there's one result per row.

You've likely done this already. In Excel, =SUMPRODUCT(B2:C2, $F$2:$F$3) filled down a column computes Xw row by row. =MMULT(B2:C4, F2:F3) does it in one step. In Python with NumPy, it's X @ w. A linear regression's predictions are exactly this product: each row holds one case's features and w holds the coefficients.
